Normalcy Alignment Moves

When you activate normalcy mode, the normalcy axis rotates to the necessary absolute angle that is calculated by the active NORMALCY LEFT mode or NORMALCY RIGHT mode. This normalcy alignment occurs during the first move after normalcy mode is activated and also occurs between consecutive non-tangent moves. The normalcy axis moves in the direction that results in the shortest move to the absolute angle. In the following figure, when you activate NORMALCY LEFT at Point A, the normalcy axis rotates to the nearest multiple of -90°. For example, if the initial angle of the normalcy axis is 100°, the normalcy axis rotates to 270°. In contrast, if the initial angle of the normalcy axis is 80°, the normalcy axis rotates to -90°.

Use the NORMALCY LEFT RELATIVE command or NORMALCY RIGHT RELATIVE command to add necessary offsets to the normalcy angles to prevent an initial alignment move. For example, if you activate NORMALCY LEFT RELATIVE at 280°, then the actual angle after Point A is 280° and the actual angle after Point B is 190°.

The normalcy alignment move uses the axis speed and axis ramping, not the task speed and task ramping. Refer to Acceleration and Deceleration Ramping Overview for more information.

Figure: Normalcy Motion Illustration

Normalcy motion (normalcy alignment moves and normalcy concurrent moves) only occurs during or between coordinated moves.

Note: If you are in VELOCITY ON mode and an alignment move is necessary between two commanded moves, the controller causes the axes to decelerate so that the normalcy axis moves smoothly. Refer to Deceleration Caused by Normalcy Mode for more information.
